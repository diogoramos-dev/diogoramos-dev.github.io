Congratulations! You’ve just been hired as the company’s new Salesforce Architect. You already have a lot of ideas about how to improve their Salesforce implementation and showcase your skills. You feel excited and ready to start delivering results right away. During the interviews, you learned about their current system and what they expect from you, so of course you don’t want to waste any time.

Right?

Not so fast!

Whether you’re joining a new company or taking on a new client through a consultancy, there’s one question you need to ask before you start building anything.

The question is: **Why Salesforce?**

There are many reasons why a company decides to adopt Salesforce.

They might need a CRM and chose the best one. Or maybe they wanted a system that is easy to customize. Some companies adopt Salesforce because big players use it and they want to position themselves at the same level. Others look for standard features that come out of the box with one of the Clouds. Some are looking to engage partners or clients through Community or Experience Cloud. And more recently, many are choosing Salesforce to take advantage of its AI capabilities.

Understanding why the company chose Salesforce and what goals they are trying to achieve with it is essential. That will be the foundation for all your solutions and work.

If your company chose Salesforce because they want to deliver new things fast, then you shouldn’t build overly customized solutions with lots of code and complex architecture that is hard to understand and maintain. Instead, take advantage of low-code features and build simple solutions that support the company’s goals, allowing you to ship new features quickly.

If your company has very specific business requirements and a team of skilled developers, then simple low-code solutions might not be enough. In this case, it makes sense to invest in more customized, complex solutions that fully meet the unique needs of the business.

Over time, goals can change. What was once a small workflow may now support a growing business with many more clients. That’s why the question **Why Salesforce** should be revisited, so your solutions can evolve and scale accordingly.

In the end, the key is alignment. As a Salesforce Architect, your role isn’t to complete tasks or show off your skills. Your real job is to build solutions that move the company closer to its goals.

Adopting the Salesforce Architect Mindset starts with understanding the reason behind the company’s choice. Salesforce represents a major investment for any business. It brings tremendous value, but only if its use is tied to a clear purpose. Once you know why Salesforce was chosen, you can make better decisions, prioritize work effectively, and ensure every solution you deliver aligns with the company’s goals.
