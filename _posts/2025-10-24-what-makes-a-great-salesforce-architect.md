Great Salesforce architects, maybe you’ve worked with some of them, maybe you are one of them, but have you ever stopped to notice what they all have in common? What do they do that makes them so great? What’s the essence that all outstanding Salesforce architects share?

In my experience, I can say that all great architects demonstrate the same habits. Of course, at different levels, but these habits are part of their core. Most excellent architects also have years of experience, which is extremely valuable, but you don’t need to wait for years to become great yourself. You can model those top professionals, adopt the same habits they have, and you’ll definitely be on the right path.

These are the 7 habits that make great Salesforce architects stand out.

## 1. Document Everything

Great architects are always building documentation. They recognize its value and invest time not only in creating it but also in making it as clear and effective as possible. If a picture is worth a thousand words, a well-written document is worth much more.

There are many types of documentation, from ARDs to Data Models, from Integration Architectures to Best Practice Guidelines; everything should be documented. What truly matters is that every decision made by an architect should be reflected in the development process, and documentation is essential to turn those ideas into something tangible.

Documentation is also critical because people come and go from companies and projects. A well-crafted document can serve as the perfect reference in these transitions, capturing all the knowledge needed for new team members to get up to speed.

If you know an architect who is highly skilled technically but doesn’t create documentation for the team, I’m sorry, but that person is not acting like an architect and is not thinking as part of a team. They’re simply making the project more dependent on their presence. What happens if they leave? Will the team be able to keep things running smoothly? Will you still understand the reasoning behind past decisions?

Documentation is valuable and should be treated with care. Don’t underestimate its power, and start making it a part of your daily routine.

## 2. Build with Security in Mind

The security of a Salesforce project has never been more important than it is today. Just look at the recent data breach incidents that have dominated the news in the past few weeks.

Even though Salesforce provides many built-in security and sharing mechanisms, we must admit that it’s not an easy system to configure. With so many tools and options available, defining a truly robust and secure architecture can be challenging. (Maybe [this](/blog/10-best-practices-for-salesforce-sharing/) article will help.)

That’s why great architects always think about security first. They build security-first solutions and prioritize system protection from the start. This mindset is crucial, especially as Orgs grow. If past solutions weren’t designed with security in mind, adding new layers of protection later becomes much harder. The business team won’t want to wait; they’ll want to replicate what was already working before.

As a Salesforce architect, it’s your mission to advocate for security mechanisms, to make your Org more reliable and risk-free. Even if clients seem unwilling to wait for architectural changes, remember that if something happens to the data in your environment, you’ll be the one held accountable.

So don’t postpone it. Don’t leave it for the next sprint. Security is the number one thing every Salesforce architect should think about. Adopt this habit right away, before the next data breach happens in your Org.

## 3. Promote Best Practices

Even though many of the best practices for Salesforce development have already been widely discussed, we should never forget a few things: new developers join the ecosystem every day, and you might be working with one of them; many professionals come from different backgrounds, like that Java developer who became a Salesforce developer (I’m sure you’ve met one of those); some best practices need to be repeated, since not every environment allows professionals to apply everything they’ve learned; and different environments require different sets of best practices. While some Salesforce best practices are universal, others are specific to certain contexts, and those in particular need to be promoted.

Great Salesforce architects never forget the importance of promoting best practices. They sidestep the Curse of Knowledge, understanding that what is obvious to them must be clearly explained to their team. If they care about ensuring that the solutions they design are implemented properly and with quality, they must make sure their teams know and follow the best practices. Only then does an architect’s work become truly effective. After all, what’s the point of designing the best architecture in the world if the developer writes inefficient code which ignores those principles?

Average architects know the best practices. Good architects document them. But great architects promote them. Be one of the great ones, start promoting best practices today, and your projects will become better because of it.

## 4. Teach and Mentor Others

Throughout my career, I’ve seen quite a few architects who didn’t communicate well with their teams. They saw themselves as superior, didn’t like to be disturbed, and never made time to explain or teach others. And what did that cause? A tense environment where people were afraid to ask questions. Team members tried to figure things out on their own, made assumptions, and sometimes, even when they knew a solution wasn’t the best one, they still followed what the architect had designed just to avoid bothering them.

Great architects don’t just make themselves available to help or answer questions, which is already great, but they also make a point of dedicating part of their time to teaching their teammates and mentoring the junior members of the team. They know that a team isn’t made of one person and that it takes several developers to build and bring to life a project designed by an architect.

Great architects also understand that team members come and go all the time, but if they don’t foster a healthy and supportive environment, that turnover will become even more frequent, making it harder for the team to evolve into a high-performing unit. They know that a team needs time to mature and grow before it can perform at an excellent level, and constant rotation only slows down this process.

Great architects care about their teams. They invest time in helping others grow and take pride in seeing their teammates advance in their careers. They understand that reaching excellence takes time, but with shared wisdom and mentorship, that journey can be accelerated.

Stop being just another technically skilled person. Choose to be a team player, someone who doesn’t just add but multiplies. Adopt the practice of teaching and mentoring, and you’ll be seen as a real force within your company, someone everyone enjoys working with. This will build trust and morale, create a more positive atmosphere, and ultimately lead to better and higher-quality results.

## 5. Keep the Org Healthy

Salesforce Orgs can easily turn chaotic. Because of how the platform works and allows for endless customization, too many objects get created, fields pop up for everything, rules exist that no one understands, automations are scattered across flows and classes, and permissions are spread through thousands of profiles and hundreds of permission sets. You get the idea.

How a Salesforce instance reaches this state is a topic for another time, but the truth is this: it’s far more common than the exception. Large Orgs tend to be messy rather than organized. And as a Salesforce Architect, it’s your responsibility to make the Org a healthier, more structured environment.

Why? Because you don’t want the problems caused by this chaos to become a crack in the foundation of your architecture. You don’t want limits to be hit under your watch, teams to be blocked from delivering improvements, or users to face bugs that damage their experience, or even cause financial loss.

Great architects understand the importance of keeping Orgs clean and healthy, staying within limits, and following established guidelines. They know they can’t fix every issue overnight, but they prioritize problems and address them gradually, aiming for continuous improvement. They also understand that preventing new issues is just as important as solving existing ones, so they stay alert to anything that could become future chaos.

Maintaining a perfect Org is hard, maybe even impossible, but great architects know there’s only one way to get close to it: by trying, intentionally. They don’t postpone cleanup. They don’t wait for a massive “technical debt reduction” project. They make it part of their daily routine, understanding that maintaining an Org is like maintaining a house: you don’t clean it once a year, you uphold its cleanliness every single day.

Don’t wait for the mess to pile up. Start now. Make keeping your Org healthy a habit.

## 6. Collaborate Across Teams

Great Salesforce architects understand that a Salesforce Org is only one part of a much larger system. It’s often integrated with other platforms: ERPs, data lakes, partner systems, and more. They also know that Salesforce impacts many teams, not only those who use it directly but also others who interact with those teams or rely on Salesforce data.

That’s why it’s essential to understand the context of other teams. It’s not enough to care only about your immediate stakeholders or end users. Great architects take the time to see the bigger picture, where Salesforce fits into the company’s broader ecosystem and how it influences other teams.

They don’t work in isolation. They actively seek to understand other perspectives, systems, and workflows, aiming to make Salesforce a system that works for everyone. Collaboration is key: knowing how users operate day to day, what managers need from reports, and how other systems expect data to be structured. Only then can Salesforce truly deliver value to the business.

Companies don’t operate in silos. They’re complex networks of interconnected software, tools, and platforms. They’re made up of different teams, each contributing in its own way toward a shared goal.

Start collaborating across teams. Learn how their systems and contexts connect. Don’t isolate yourself. Make your solution part of something bigger. Turn this into a habit and start now.

## 7. Stay Curious and Keep Learning

Three releases a year, new clouds being launched, companies being acquired. Salesforce keeps evolving and expanding its products. Technology moves fast: new tools, platforms, and frameworks appear all the time, and software development never stops changing.

With so many updates, it’s hard to keep up with everything. Great Salesforce architects know this, which is why they make learning a habit. They stay curious and open to discovering new things, always alert to what could be revolutionary in their field.

They don’t wait for a technology to stabilize, for a demand to arise, or for a formal course to become available. They’re constantly exploring new ideas that can improve their daily work. They test new features, build proofs of concept, and experiment with innovations that could benefit their clients.

They don’t wait for someone to tell them what to learn. They actively seek knowledge because learning has become second nature to them. They stay curious and hungry for new tools, whether to solve a problem or make a process easier.

The internet is full of solutions to almost every challenge. It’s very unlikely you’ll face a problem no one has faced before. Be curious. Search for information. Stay open to new ideas and alert to what might be useful. Make learning a habit, and you’ll start seeing far more solutions than problems.

## Conclusion

Average architects remember some of these habits from time to time. Good architects practice them occasionally. But great Salesforce architects have already internalized them. These habits are natural and part of who they are.

Great architects don’t think about these disciplines only during tough times or career reflections. They carry them at their core and use them as tools to create high-quality solutions that meet real customer needs, stay connected with the company’s systems, and help their teams grow and perform at their best.

Adopt the seven habits. Take it one step at a time. These habits are the bedrock of excellence. Commit to them, and your work will no longer be measured by what you build, but by the lasting, collaborative, and secure ecosystem you ultimately create.
