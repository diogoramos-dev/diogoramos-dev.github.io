Imagine this: you’ve just been hired as the new Salesforce Architect for a company. It’s your first days on the job, you’ve already asked why Salesforce (see more [here](/blog/first-question-when-joining-a-new-company/)), and you’re ready to start delivering results. What do you do? Wait for orders from your superior? Wait for customer requests?

Well, if you want to act as a true architect, you should define your agenda, or at least part of it. You possess the knowledge and experience to anticipate what can go wrong in Salesforce orgs. You’ve seen many errors repeated and have already gathered your preferred set of best practices.

So, don’t wait for others to tell you what to do. Be the guardian of the org, act proactively, and take control of the mess before the mess takes control of you.

Here are five things you should do when joining a new company or project.

## 1. Check the Org Health

You don’t want to start implementing new projects in an org that’s riddled with problems and technical debt. You first need to understand the current health of the environment before adding anything new.

Salesforce Optimizer, Health Check, Salesforce Code Analyzer, and many other third-party apps can be used for this task. The tool selection will depend on the characteristics of your org. If it doesn’t have many code customizations, you could skip Code Analyzer; if it relies heavily on Experience Cloud pages, you could use the Page Optimizer plugin.

After a thorough analysis, you should have a list of the most concerning items that need to be addressed. You can then define the priorities for each technical debt and build a plan to resolve them.

## 2. Understand the Company’s Goals

There’s only one way you can truly contribute to your company’s success: by helping it reach its goals. You could build the best solution in the world, but it won’t matter if it isn’t aligned with the company’s objectives.

Take some time to talk with people in management positions, your leaders, and your coworkers to get a clear picture of the main goals at different levels. Having these goals in mind when designing solutions will be incredibly helpful, as it will give you clarity and make decisions easier than ever.

Also, understand their goals for you. Why did they hire you? What do they expect from you? Understand their expectations for you over the next 3, 6, and 12 months. This kind of alignment is essential to ensure you’re doing what you should be doing.

## 3. Understand the Business and the Industry

The first thing you should try to understand is what makes your company unique. What are the main products and services that set it apart? Established products usually require less risky strategies, while new ones may allow for more innovative approaches.

Research the industry. You don’t need to be an expert, but you should at least understand your customers. Do they use specific terms you don’t recognize? Look them up. By speaking the same language as your customers, you’ll better understand their requests.

Also, study the industry your company operates in. Who are the main competitors? What’s the current economic scenario for this sector? Are there any new regulations that could impact your company? Again, you don’t need to be an expert, but you need to understand the basics. As an architect, you can’t be asking clients about fundamental topics. It will make you seem unprepared.

## 4. Talk with Users

You’ve probably already seen how things are implemented in the org and have an opinion on some aspects. Some you liked, but others you think could be done differently. You may already have ideas for changes you want to make, but have you considered the users? Are you sure they’ll like your ideas?

Before changing anything, talk to the users. Conduct interviews, understand what they like and dislike in the current environment. If you really want to make a difference, your focus should be on making end users happy.

End-user feedback is one of the most valuable resources for building effective solutions. Remember, Salesforce is complex, and many features can be implemented in different ways. Listening to your users will help you choose the best option for them.

## 5. Define Your First Goal

With a comprehensive understanding of the organization, its technical debt, company goals, business, industry, and end-user perspectives, you are now equipped to define your initial objective.

It is crucial to establish your first goal only after completing these foundational steps. Prioritize what is most urgent, identify potential quick wins, and determine what can be deferred. Base this decision on all previous discussions.

Having gathered all necessary information, you can now confidently make decisions, set priorities, and strategically navigate the business landscape.
